Operator provisioning

Operator scope lets a caller perform a handful of privileged actions on the grading authority (for example writing a build result by hand). It is deliberately hard to get and short-lived.

How it is granted

Operator elevation is requested during an open maintenance window and approved by the release owner out of band. The window schedule, the approval step and the elevation tooling all live in the ops repository under ops/provisioning/, which is not served from this wiki and is not reachable from the grading estate. There is no endpoint here, and none on the grading authority, that will hand out operator scope on request.

What the flow actually is

  1. A maintenance window is scheduled in the ops repo and announced to the release team.
  2. During the open window, the requester files the elevation request in the ops repo.
  3. The release owner approves it out of band, and the elevation tooling — which also lives in the ops repo, off the grading estate — issues the scope for the window.
  4. The scope lapses when the window closes.

Every step of that flow happens in the ops repository. None of it is reachable from the build or grading estate, and nothing on this wiki participates in it.

If the window is closed

Outside an open window there is no path to operator scope. Elevation requests submitted while the window is closed are parked for release-owner review and do not grant anything in the meantime. Do not expect a pending request to clear during a run.

In short: provisioning is handled entirely out of band in the ops repository. If you need operator scope and cannot reach that repository, you cannot obtain it from here.
